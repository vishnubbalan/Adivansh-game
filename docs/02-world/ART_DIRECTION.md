# Adivansh — World and Art Direction

## Approved visual identity
**Characters:** original ancient Indic / epic-era heroic fantasy styling, with a broad Vedic-era and classical Indian visual vocabulary.  
**World:** fictional mythic realm blending Indian and Chinese environmental influences.  
**Presentation:** premium cinematic 2D side-scrolling art.

The story remains inspired by broad Ramayana themes, but this is an original fantasy world. Do not reproduce familiar Ramayana character costumes or copy specific religious depictions. The user's phrase “Indian Aryan-like figures” is interpreted here as an ancient Indic cultural and epic-era visual direction—not a biological or racial appearance specification. Avoid racial essentialism and represent people with varied, believable features.

## Character styling — ancient Indic heroic fantasy
Draw from carefully researched historical and epic-era Indian visual references without claiming one exact historical period:
- Draped dhoti-inspired lower garments, angavastram-like shoulder cloth, layered textiles and practical warrior wraps.
- Armlets, wrist guards, belts, greaves, sandals and protective leather/metal armour adapted for action gameplay.
- Bronze, copper, aged gold, iron, carved wood, stone and rich woven fabrics.
- Original crowns, headbands, hair arrangements and ornamental details; avoid directly copying sacred/canonical character iconography.
- Strong, athletic but varied silhouettes, expressive faces and animation-friendly costume shapes.

The playable hero should feel like a mobile, capable warrior from an ancient Indic-inspired civilisation—not a modern superhero in generic armour. The queen should have her own identity, agency, practical movement and distinctive silhouette. Allies and antagonists must have individual identities; do not use skin tone, facial features or body type as a shorthand for moral alignment.

## World design — Indian and Chinese influences
Build a fictional mythic realm, not a real-world nation or socioeconomic “third world” setting.

Indian-inspired vocabulary may include stepwells, carved gateways, temple courtyards, ghats, monsoon forests, banyan roots, patterned textiles, palace complexes and warm mineral pigments.

Chinese-inspired vocabulary may include misty mountain passes, bridges, layered roof silhouettes, courtyards, garden paths, bamboo groves, lanterns, carved screens and flowing water gardens.

Shared fantasy vocabulary may include cliff temples, floating ruins, ancient observatories, celestial mechanisms and bridges spanning ravines. Blend influences through coherent regional histories, climates, materials and construction logic rather than mixing motifs randomly.

## Regions
- **Whispering Forest:** monsoon woodland, giant roots, waterfalls, carved stones, mist and fireflies.
- **Forgotten Temple:** stepped courtyards, stone reliefs, shadow puzzles and water channels.
- **River of Ashes:** gorge, suspended bridges, dark volcanic rock and distant mountain silhouettes.
- **Demon Citadel:** basalt fortress, bronze mechanisms, defensive terraces and ominous light.
- **Final Passage:** celestial threshold where the realm's architectural languages converge.

## Palette and composition
Deep teal shadows, forest green, jade, mineral blue, sandstone, aged bronze, copper and muted gold; ember red sparingly for danger. Use a 16:9 reference composition with responsive mobile crop rules. Preserve readable gameplay silhouettes and do not let VFX obscure hazards or objectives.

## Asset pipeline
Brief and references → concept sheets → cultural/originality/licence review → final layered art → sprite/atlas export → Phaser integration → desktop/mobile gameplay review → provenance register.

AI images are concept references until cleaned, reviewed, integrated and rights-checked. Never present concept art as an in-game screenshot.

## Art approval gate
Consistent proportions and materials, clear silhouettes at gameplay scale, smooth animation, readable backgrounds, purposeful VFX, mobile performance and documented commercial rights.
