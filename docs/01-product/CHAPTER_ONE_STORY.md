# Chapter One — The Last Harvest Festival

**Season:** The Whispering Forest · **Chapter:** 1 of 100  
**Status:** Narrative production draft and first playable implementation scope  
**Primary emotion:** Joy and belonging, with a quiet thread of unease  
**Playable location:** Veyra, the river-side royal village  
**Estimated first-play duration:** 8–12 minutes  
**Chapter promise:** Let the player experience the peace that Adivansh and Meera will later fight to restore.

## 1. Narrative purpose

Chapter One is not a combat level. It is the emotional foundation of the whole adventure. The player must meet the people of Veyra, understand that Adivansh is a capable but approachable royal protector, and see Meera Devi as a respected, active leader rather than a prize to be rescued.

The harvest festival celebrates a successful season after difficult monsoon rains. The village is alive with music, craft, food, games, lanterns and shared work. Adivansh arrives without a royal procession and joins the celebrations. Meera has chosen to spend the day among the people, listening to their concerns and helping settle a small dispute over the distribution of grain.

The chapter ends at dusk when a messenger arrives from the southern watch. He carries no full explanation—only a warning that unfamiliar armed riders have been seen near the forest border. Meera recognises a detail in the report that unsettles her. She does not explain it yet. The player leaves the chapter feeling that the world is precious, not that it is already lost.

## 2. Canon and continuity

- Adivansh is the playable Seeker: a disciplined young royal warrior who prefers to be among the people.
- Meera Devi is the Queen: intelligent, observant, decisive and trusted by the community. She has agency, opinions and responsibilities beyond her relationship with Adivansh.
- The village is Veyra. It is an original fictional settlement; do not present it as a real historical place.
- The festival's golden harvest ribbons are ordinary woven decorations. They are not yet the Golden Thread clue from the later forest chapters.
- No demon, abduction, burning village, boss or combat encounter occurs in this chapter.
- The messenger's warning foreshadows Chapter Two, **Shadows in the Court**. Chapter Three, **Fire at Midnight**, is the later inciting catastrophe.
- The existing forest prototype is a later forest pursuit segment. Do not relabel it as this festival chapter or delete it while Chapter One is built.

## 3. Opening cinematic — “A village that remembers how to sing”

**Time:** Late afternoon, warm sunlight after rain.

Black screen. We hear a river, a hand drum, children laughing and a distant flute. A single line appears:

> “Before the road became a search, it was a place called home.”

The camera fades into Veyra's festival square. Cloth streamers cross the street. Farmers unload baskets of grain. A potter turns a wheel beneath a canopy. Children chase each other between flower garlands. A small river glints beyond the market roofs. The palace is visible on a hill, but it is not the centre of the composition; the people are.

Adivansh enters from the left, walking rather than riding. His sword remains sheathed. A child recognises him and waves. He returns the greeting and helps lift a basket that has slipped from a cart.

**Adivansh (thought):** “The rains were kind this year. Let the people have one day without worry.”

The player gains control.

## 4. Main story sequence

### Beat A — Join the celebration

**Objective:** “Walk through the square and greet the festival.”

The first section teaches horizontal movement in a safe, wide lane. The player sees activity before being asked to interact. The camera reveals the square in layers: foreground banners, the crowd, market stalls, river and distant palace.

**Environmental storytelling:**
- Grain baskets are full, but a few are deliberately set aside for households affected by the late monsoon.
- A repaired bridge model at the children's stall hints at the village's communal resilience.
- Guards are present but relaxed; weapons are lowered.
- Meera is visible in the middle distance speaking with villagers, not waiting passively for the hero.

### Beat B — The potter's small problem

**NPC:** Dev, village potter.  
**Objective:** “Help Dev steady the festival lantern stand.”

A gust of wind has tilted a lantern stand. Dev asks Adivansh to hold it while he secures the base. The player presses Interact in range. A short animation or visual response shows the stand settling; lanterns glow.

**Dev:** “A steady hand. Just what a festival needs.”  
**Adivansh:** “The hands that made these deserve the thanks.”

This interaction introduces proximity-based interaction and demonstrates the hero's humility. No item collection or inventory system is required.

### Beat C — A little festival challenge

**NPC:** Two children, Tara and Kavi.  
**Objective:** “Take part in the ribbon leap.”

The children invite Adivansh to jump over three low flower-and-ribbon hurdles set along a safe lane. This is a playful contest, not a combat tutorial. The game teaches jump timing and landing. Each hurdle has a clear silhouette, generous clearance and no damage penalty. Touch controls must be able to complete it.

**Tara:** “You can cross the whole lane without touching a ribbon?”  
**Adivansh:** “I can try. But you must judge the landing.”

On the third clean jump, the children cheer. A small burst of petals marks success. If the player misses a hurdle, the attempt resets gently; no health is lost and no progress is blocked.

### Beat D — The grain keeper's question

**NPC:** Ila, keeper of the harvest stores.  
**Objective:** “Hear Ila's news.”

Ila thanks Meera for ensuring that grain is shared fairly. She tells Adivansh that the Queen has spent the morning listening to families and resolving a disagreement about the river stores.

**Ila:** “She could have sent an order from the palace. Instead, she came and listened.”  
**Adivansh:** “That is why they trust her.”

This is the first direct character beat that establishes Meera's leadership.

### Beat E — Meera among her people

**Objective:** “Speak with Meera near the festival dais.”

Meera is reviewing the village's harvest ledger with two elders. She notices Adivansh before he reaches her, finishes the conversation, and approaches him. She is warm but not ornamental; her attention is divided between the festival and the people depending on her.

**Meera:** “You came without the royal escort.”  
**Adivansh:** “I was invited by the village. I thought I should arrive as a villager.”  
**Meera:** “Then you have already done better than half the court.”

They share a brief private moment. Meera asks whether the southern road is still passable after the rains. Adivansh says the old stone markers are holding, but the forest road has become difficult. Meera says the forest should be treated with respect, not fear.

A bell rings. The sun is lowering. The festival begins its lantern lighting.

### Beat F — The messenger at dusk

The music slows. A rider enters the square at speed, dismounts badly and catches himself against a post. His cloak is wet with river spray and road mud. The villagers fall quiet.

**Messenger:** “My Queen. A report from the southern watch.”  
**Meera:** “You rode through the rain. Speak.”  
**Messenger:** “Three unfamiliar companies were seen beyond the old boundary stones. They did not take the road. They moved along the forest edge.”

Meera studies the messenger's map. Her expression changes at one detail: a boundary marker has been turned to face inward. She folds the map before the crowd can see it.

**Adivansh:** “Do we know who they are?”  
**Meera:** “Not yet. And we will not frighten the village with guesses.”

She asks the captain to quietly double the southern watch and tells the festival to continue. Then, privately:

**Meera:** “At first light, I want the old border records. Something about this report does not fit.”  
**Adivansh:** “I will come with you.”  
**Meera:** “I expected you would.”

The camera holds on the lanterns rising into the dusk. One lantern drifts toward the dark tree line. Cut to black.

**Chapter-end title:** “CHAPTER I — THE LAST HARVEST FESTIVAL”  
**Next:** “Chapter II — Shadows in the Court”

## 5. Character direction

### Adivansh
- Starts relaxed, observant and comfortable among ordinary people.
- Uses humour lightly; does not boast about combat or royal status.
- His strength is shown through helpful actions and careful movement.
- The chapter should establish the emotional contrast between this peaceful day and the later loss.

### Meera Devi
- Initiates conversations and makes decisions.
- Is respected because of visible actions: listening, allocating grain fairly and responding calmly to the warning.
- Her private exchange with Adivansh shows mutual trust, not dependence.
- Her recognition of the altered boundary marker is a deliberate seed for Chapter Two.

### Village cast
- **Dev:** potter; practical, warm and proud of his craft.
- **Tara and Kavi:** children; energetic, playful and fair-minded.
- **Ila:** harvest-store keeper; direct, perceptive and protective of vulnerable families.
- **Messenger:** exhausted, professional and worried; not a comic panic device.
- **Southern watch captain:** can be introduced visually now and given dialogue in Chapter Two.

## 6. Gameplay and level design

### Critical path
1. Spawn at the western festival entrance.
2. Walk through the square; learn horizontal movement.
3. Interact with Dev's lantern stand.
4. Complete three low ribbon jumps with Tara and Kavi.
5. Speak with Ila.
6. Speak with Meera at the dais.
7. Watch the messenger scene and chapter-end beat.

### Controls
- Move: A/D or left/right.
- Jump: Space/W/up.
- Interact: E or on-screen E.
- No attack button is needed in this chapter; hide or visually de-emphasise attack if the shared HUD supports it without disrupting other chapters.

### Accessibility and failure handling
- Interaction prompts use icon + text, not colour alone.
- NPC interaction radius is generous and consistent.
- Jump hurdles have visible approach space and forgiving collision bounds.
- Missing a jump resets only that hurdle attempt; no health penalty.
- Dialogue remains readable at 390px viewport width and can be advanced with Interact.
- Pause, resize and scene restart must not lose objective state or trap the player.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences; do not require fast repeated inputs.

## 7. Audio and visual direction

- Warm late-afternoon palette: honey gold, terracotta, indigo cloth, leaf green and river blue.
- Festival set dressing: handwoven banners, flower garlands, pottery, grain baskets, oil lamps, carved timber stalls and simple musical instruments.
- Architecture should follow the approved fictional world style; avoid random sacred symbols or direct copies of real temple iconography.
- Ambient layers: river, birds, crowd murmur, hand drums, flute and small bells.
- Dialogue ducks the music slightly. At messenger arrival, remove most crowd layers and let the river and one low drum remain.
- Avoid a sudden horror sting; the warning should create unease, not reveal the later attack.

## 8. Quest state and acceptance criteria

Suggested session flags:
- `dev-helped`
- `ribbon-jumps-complete`
- `ila-spoken-to`
- `meera-spoken-to`
- `messenger-warning-heard`
- `chapter-one-complete`

Completion requires the first four player-facing activities and the messenger sequence. All completion events must fire once only.

**Definition of Done**
- The chapter is playable from the title screen through the messenger scene on desktop and mobile.
- Keyboard and touch movement, jump and interaction both work.
- No combat is required or accidentally triggered.
- All required NPC interactions are reachable and trigger once.
- The ribbon challenge is forgiving and cannot soft-lock.
- Dialogue and objectives remain legible at desktop and narrow mobile sizes.
- Chapter completion is recorded once; the next-chapter tease is explicit and does not imply Chapter Two is playable.
- Unit tests cover chapter progression and repeat-interaction protection.
- Playwright covers start, core movement, all interactions, completion, mobile viewport and restart.
- Human playtest records confusion points and completion time before calling the chapter polished.

## 9. Out of scope for this first implementation slice

- Chapter Three attack and abduction cinematic.
- Combat, weapons, enemy AI, inventory, save slots and online services.
- Final commissioned character art, voice acting and full soundtrack.
- Any claim that later chapters are playable.
