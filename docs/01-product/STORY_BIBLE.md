# Adivansh — Story and Narrative Bible

## Narrative identity
An original mythic fantasy adventure inspired by broad Ramayana themes and emotional structure. It is not a scene-by-scene retelling and should not reproduce canonical character designs, costumes or sacred iconography as visual shorthand. Use original names, kingdoms, characters and lore.

## Core premise
A young royal warrior returns to find that the queen has been taken by a force moving through fractured kingdoms. A trail of strange golden fibres leads into the Whispering Forest, protected by an ancient guardian. Defeating it reveals that the abduction is connected to an older power beneath forgotten temples.

As the journey continues, the hero discovers that the queen was taken because she holds knowledge that could prevent the realm's ancient powers from being misused. The hero must decide whom to trust, what sacrifices are justified and how to unite rival cultures against a shared threat. This is a proposed direction; names, motivations and twists require narrative approval.

## Themes and tone
Loyalty, courage with restraint, friendship across cultures, responsibility, sacrifice, guardianship and hope. Tone: epic, adventurous, mysterious and emotionally grounded. Avoid parody or stereotypes of living cultures and religions.

## Character roles
- **The Seeker:** playable royal warrior, determined and skilled but still learning.
- **The Queen:** active character with agency, knowledge and choices.
- **The Guardian:** early boss whose defeat reveals the first clue.
- **The Guide:** scholar or wanderer who interprets clues.
- **The Antagonist:** seeks to control the realm's ancient power; motivation should be understandable.
- **Allies and rivals:** distinct goals, abilities and regional identities.

Create a character catalogue with approved names, biography, motivation, dialogue voice and visual reference before final production assets.

## Chapter arc
1. **The Whispering Forest:** learn movement and combat, face the guardian and recover the golden clue.
2. **The Forgotten Temple:** explore an ancient complex, solve light/shadow mechanisms and face sentinels.
3. **The River of Ashes:** cross a gorge, help a messenger and learn more about the queen's destination.
4. **The Demon Citadel:** infiltrate a stronghold, face elite guards and uncover the antagonist's plan.
5. **The Final Passage:** confront the antagonist and resolve the future of the realm.

These are planned chapters, not claims of implemented content.

## Narrative delivery
Use short in-engine scenes, concise dialogue, environmental storytelling, collectible lore and boss introductions. Optional generated dialogue must never alter canonical plot facts, quest flags or progression.

## Cultural and originality guardrails
Use the Ramayana as thematic inspiration, not as permission to copy sacred depictions. Do not treat Indian, Chinese or Greek cultures as interchangeable decoration. Research visual motifs and architecture; avoid sacred symbols or religious figures as casual enemy props or loot. Keep the world fictional and coherent.

Each chapter must define its player objective, emotional beat, escalation, climax, resolution, required/optional information, quest-state transitions, dialogue and continuity checks.
